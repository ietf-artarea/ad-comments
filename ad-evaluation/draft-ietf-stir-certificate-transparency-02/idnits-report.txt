12	              draft-ietf-stir-certificate-transparency-02

14	Abstract

16	   This document describes a framework for the use of the Certificate
17	   Transparency (CT) protocol for publicly logging the existence of
18	   Secure Telephone Identity (STI) certificates as they are issued or
19	   observed.  This allows any interested party that is part of the STI
20	   eco-system to audit STI certification authority (CA) activity and
21	   audit both the issuance of suspect certificates and the certificate
22	   logs themselves.  The intent is for the establishment of a level of
23	   trust in the STI eco-system that depends on the verification of
24	   telephone numbers requiring and refusing to honor STI certificates
25	   that do not appear in a established log.  This effectively
26	   establishes the precedent that STI CAs must add all issued
27	   certificates to the logs and thus establishes unique association of
28	   STI certificates to an authorized provider or assignee of a telephone
29	   number resource.  The primary role of CT in the STI ecosystem is for
30	   verifiable trust in the avoidance of issuance of unauthorized
31	   duplicate telephone number level delegate certificates or provider
32	   level certificates.  This provides a robust auditable mechanism for
33	   the detection of unauthorized creation of certificate credentials for
34	   illegitimate spoofing of telephone numbers or service provider codes
35	   (SPC).

37	   The framework borrows the log structure and API model from RFC6962 to
38	   enable public auditing and verifiability of certificate issuance.
39	   While the foundational mechanisms for log operation, Merkle Tree
40	   construction, and Signed Certificate Timestamps (SCTs) are aligned
41	   with RFC6962, this document contextualizes their application in the
42	   STIR eco-system, focusing on verifiable control over telephone number
43	   or service provider code resources.

140	1.  Introduction

142	   Certificate Transparency (CT) aims to mitigate the problem of mis-
143	   issued certificates by providing append-only logs of issued
144	   certificates.  The logs do not themselves prevent mis-issuance, but
145	   ensure that interested parties (particularly those named in
146	   legitimate certificates or certificate chains) can detect such mis-
147	   issuance.  [RFC6962] describes the core protocols and mechanisms for
148	   use of CT for the purposes of public TLS server certificates
149	   associated with a domain name as part of the public domain name
150	   system (DNS).  This document describes a conceptually similar
151	   framework that directly borrows concepts like transparency receipts
152	   in the form of SCTs and how they are used in certificates and its
153	   specific use as part of the larger STIR framework for call
154	   authentication.  This framework is defined for the specific use with
155	   both Secure Telephone Identity (STI) certificates [RFC8226] and
156	   delegate certificates [RFC9060].

158	   Telephone numbers (TNs) and their management and assignment by
159	   telephone service providers and Responsible Organizations (RespOrgs)
160	   for toll-free numbers share many similarities to the Domain Name
161	   System (DNS) where there is a global uniqueness and established
162	   association of telephone numbers to regulatory jurisdictions that
163	   manage the allocation and assignment of telephone numbers under
164	   country codes and a set of numeric digits for routing telephone calls
165	   and messages over telephone networks.  STI Certificates use a
166	   TNAuthList extension defined in [RFC8226] to specifically associate
167	   either telephone service providers or telephone numbers to the
168	   issuance of STI certificates and certificate change that are intended
169	   to represent the authorized right to use a telephone number.  This
170	   trusted association can be establish via mechanisms such as Authority
171	   tokens for TNAuthList defined in [RFC9448].  Certificate transparency
172	   and the concept of transparency is generally meant to provide a
173	   publicly verifiable and auditable representation of the creation of
174	   certificates in order to establish transparency and trust to
175	   interested parties as part of a stir related eco-system.

177	   There is three primary actors in the certificate transparency
178	   framework.  There is the STI Certification Authorities (CAs) that
179	   submit all certificates to be issued to one or more transparency
180	   append-only log services.  The log services are network services that
181	   implement the protocol operations for submissions of STI certificates
182	   and subsequent queries.  They are hosted by interested parties in the
183	   STI ecosystem and can accept certificate log submissions from any
184	   other CA participant.  The second role is the monitors that play the
185	   role of monitoring the CT logs to check for potential mis-issuance as
186	   well as auditing of the log services.  This role can be played by any
187	   STI ecosystem participant interested in the trust of the ecosystem or
188	   the integrity of the telephone number or provider level certificates
189	   produced in the eco-system.  CT provides a mechanism of a receipt or
190	   Signed Certificate Timestamp (SCT) that is provided as a result of
191	   submitting a certificate to the append-only log.  The third actor
192	   role in the certificate transparency framework is the eco-system
193	   participants that can send and receive receipt(s) or SCT(s) to prove
194	   and validate that a certificate was submitted to a log(s) and
195	   optionally query the log directly for further validation.

197	   The details that follow in this document will detail the specific
198	   protocols and framework for Certificate Transparency associated with
199	   STI certificates.  Most of the details borrow many of the concepts of
200	   certificate transparency defined in [RFC6962] used in web browser and
201	   web PKI environments, but provides a specific framework designed for
202	   STI certificates and their specific issuance and usage in a
203	   telecommunications and telephone number dependent eco-system.

205	   This general mechanism could also be used for transparently logging
206	   other important stir related metadata associations perhaps via
207	   JWTClaimConstraints defined in [RFC8226] and [RFC9118] or other ways
208	   defined in potential future extensions of this document.

218	3.  The Use of Certificate Transparency for STI Certificates

220	   CT log(s) contains certificate chains, which can be submitted by any
221	   CA authorized in a STIR eco-system.  It is expected that these CAs
222	   will contribute all their newly issued certificates to one or more
223	   logs.  Note, in [RFC6962] it is possible for certificate holders to
224	   directly contribute their own certificate chains or interested third
225	   parties, however because in stir eco-systems that generally consist
226	   of entities that are authorized to be assigned telephone number
227	   resources, this does not seem to be a likely scenario.  Generally,
228	   many stir eco-systems have a controlled set of CAs that are
229	   authorized to participate as valid trust anchors.  It is required
230	   that each chain ends with a trust anchor that is accepted by the log
231	   which would include those authorized trust anchors or a subset of
232	   them.  When a chain is accepted by a log, a signed timestamp is
233	   returned, which is later used to provide evidence to STIR
234	   verification services (VS), defined in [RFC8224], that the chain has
235	   been submitted.  A VS can thus require that all certificates they
236	   accept as valid are accompanied by signed timestamps.

238	   Those concerned about mis-issuance of STIR certificates can monitor
239	   the logs, asking them regularly for all new entries, and can thus
240	   check whether the service provider codes or telephone numbers for
241	   which they are responsible have had certificates issued that they did
242	   not expect.  What they do with this information, particularly when
243	   they find that a mis-issuance has happened, is beyond the scope of
244	   this document.  However, broadly speaking, because many existing STI
245	   ecosystems have a connection to regulated and industry environments
246	   that govern the issuance of STI certificates, they can invoke
247	   existing mechanisms for dealing with issues such as mis-issued
248	   certificates, such as working with the CA to get the certificate
249	   revoked or with maintainers of trust anchor lists to get the CA
250	   removed.

252	4.  Terminology

254	   This section defines key terms used throughout the STI-CT framework
255	   to ensure clarity and consistency.

257	4.1.  Authentication Service (AS)

259	   A service defined in [RFC8224] that signs the identity of a telephone
260	   call using Secure Telephone Identity (STI) certificates, ensuring the
261	   authenticity of the caller information.  It ensures that STI
262	   Certificates contain SCTs.

264	4.2.  Certificate Transparency (CT)

266	   A framework designed to provide an open and verifiable log of issued
267	   certificates.  It aims to detect and prevent the misuse or mis-
268	   issuance of certificates by maintaining append-only logs that can be
269	   audited by any interested party.

271	4.3.  Delegate Certificate

273	   A type of STI certificate defined in [RFC9060] that associates a
274	   specific telephone number or a range of telephone numbers with a
275	   particular entity used to delegate the right to use these numbers.

277	4.4.  Log

279	   An append-only, cryptographically verifiable structure used in
280	   Certificate Transparency to record pre-certificate entries.  Logs
281	   accept submissions, generate Signed Certificate Timestamps (SCTs),
282	   and maintain the integrity of the entries through a Merkle Tree
283	   structure.

285	4.5.  Merkle Tree

287	   A cryptographic data structure used in logs to ensure the integrity
288	   and consistency of the entries.  It is built by hashing individual
289	   log entries and combining them into a single root hash that
290	   represents the state of the entire log.

292	4.6.  Precertificate

294	   A certificate issued by an CA that is intended to be submitted to a
295	   Certificate Transparency log before the final certificate is issued.
296	   The pre-certificate includes a special extension (the poison
297	   extension) that prevents it from being used as a valid certificate on
298	   its own.

300	4.7.  Signed Certificate Timestamp (SCT)

302	   A data structure provided by a Certificate Transparency log in
303	   response to a pre-certificate submission.  The SCT serves as a
304	   promise from the log to include the submitted pre-certificate in the
305	   log within a specified time frame (Maximum Merge Delay).  It is
306	   included in the final certificate to prove that it has been logged.

308	4.8.  STI Certification Authority (STI-CA)

310	   An entity responsible for issuing STI certificates in the Secure
311	   Telephone Identity ecosystem.  The CA can also issue pre-
312	   certificates, which are submitted to CT logs before the final
313	   certificate is issued.

315	4.9.  STI Subordinate Certification Authority (STI-SCA)

317	   An entity authorized by an CA to issue STI certificates under the
318	   authority of the STI-CA.  The STI-SCA can also issue pre-certificates
319	   for submission to CT logs.

321	4.10.  Signed Tree Head (STH)

323	   A cryptographically signed data structure that represents the current
324	   state of a Certificate Transparency log.  It includes the root hash
325	   of the Merkle Tree and the number of entries in the log, allowing
326	   auditors to verify the integrity and consistency of the log.

328	4.11.  TBSCertificate (To Be Signed Certificate)

330	   A component of an X.509 certificate that contains all the information
331	   about the certificate except the actual digital signature.  The
332	   TBSCertificate includes fields such as the version, serial number,
333	   issuer, validity period, subject, and the subject's public key
334	   information.  This component is signed by the certificate authority
335	   (CA) to create the final certificate.  In the context of Certificate
336	   Transparency, the TBSCertificate of a pre-certificate is submitted to
337	   the log for inclusion.

339	4.12.  Verification Service (VS)

341	   A service defined in [RFC8224] that verifies the authenticity of a
342	   telephone call by checking the validity of the PASSporT token,
343	   including verification that certificate contains valid SCTs.

345	5.  STI Certificate Transparency Framework

347	   This section describes the format and operational procedures for logs
348	   in the STI Certificate Transparency (CT) framework.

350	5.1.  Log Entries

352	   Logs in the STI CT framework are append-only structures that store
353	   entries in a Merkle Tree and use SHA-256 for data hashing.  The
354	   entries consist of pre-certificates submitted by STI Certification
355	   Authorities (STI-CAs) or Subordinate Certification Authorities (STI-
356	   SCAs).  The log entries help ensure that all issued STI certificates
357	   can be audited for legitimacy.

359	5.2.  Precertificate Submission

361	   An STI-CA/STI-SCA submits a pre-certificate to a log before the
362	   actual STI certificate is issued.  The pre-certificate submission
363	   must include all necessary intermediate certificates to validate the
364	   chain up to an accepted root certificate.  The root certificate may
365	   be omitted from the submission.

367	   When a pre-certificate is submitted:

369	   *  The log verifies the chain of the pre-certificate up to a trusted
370	      root.

372	   *  If valid, the log generates and returns a Signed Certificate
373	      Timestamp (SCT) to the submitter.

375	   *  The SCT serves as a promise from the log that the pre-certificate
376	      will be included in the Merkle Tree within a defined Maximum Merge
377	      Delay (MMD).

379	   Logs must publish a list of accepted root certificates, which aligns
380	   with those trusted in the STIR ecosystem.  The inclusion of SCTs in
381	   the actual STI certificates is critical, as Verification Services
382	   (VS) will only accept certificates that include valid SCTs.

384	   Note: The data structures (e.g., LogEntry,
385	   SignedCertificateTimestamp, TreeHeadSignature) in this section follow
386	   the definitions provided in [RFC6962].

388	5.3.  Log Entry

390	   Logs may impose a limit on the length of the certificate chain they
391	   will accept.  The log verifies the validity of the pre-certificate
392	   chain up to an accepted root and, upon acceptance, stores the entire
393	   chain for future auditing.

395	5.4.  Signed Certificate Timestamp (SCT)

397	   The SCT is included in the final STI certificate, and VS services
398	   will check the presence and validity of SCTs to verify the legitimacy
399	   of the certificate.

401	5.5.  Merkle Tree Structure

403	   Logs use a Merkle Tree structure, with each leaf corresponding to a
404	   MerkleTreeLeaf entry.  The leaves are hashed to form the tree, which
405	   is continuously updated as new entries are added.

407	   The root hash of the Merkle Tree represents the state of the log at a
408	   given time and can be used to verify the inclusion of specific
409	   entries.

411	5.6.  Signed Tree Head (STH)

413	   The log periodically signs the root of the Merkle Tree, producing a
414	   Signed Tree Head (STH), which ensures the integrity of the log over
415	   time.

417	   Logs must produce an STH within the Maximum Merge Delay (MMD) to
418	   confirm that all SCTs issued have been incorporated into the Merkle
419	   Tree.  Auditors and monitors can use the STH to verify that the log
420	   is operating correctly and that no entries have been tampered with.

422	6.  STI-CT APIs

424	   STI-CT re-uses the REST endpoints defined in Section 4 of [RFC6962]
425	   (the "/ct/v1/" namespace) with no semantic changes.  For operational
426	   clarity it is RECOMMENDED deployments expose them under a path
427	   /stict/v1/ but the request/response formats are compatible with
428	   [RFC6962].

430	7.  Clients

432	   This section describes various roles clients of STI-CT perform.  Any
433	   inconsistency detected by clients could serve as evidence that a log
434	   has not behaved correctly, and the signatures on the data structures
435	   prevent the log from denying any misbehavior.

437	7.1.  Submitters (STI-CA/STI-SCA)

439	   Submitters in the STI-CT framework are typically STI Certification
440	   Authorities (STI-CAs) or Subordinate Certification Authorities (STI-
441	   SCAs).  These entities submit pre-certificates to the log as
442	   described in the APIs section.  The returned Signed Certificate
443	   Timestamp (SCT) can then be used to construct the final STI
444	   certificate, which includes one or more SCTs.

446	7.2.  Use of SCTs by Authentication and Verification Services

448	   This specification defines the STI eco-system rely exclusively on the
449	   pre-certificate chain method defined in [RFC6962]; therefore every
450	   Certificate issued for call signing MUST already carry one or more
451	   embedded SCTs in the SignedCertificateTimestampList X.509 extension
452	   at issuance time.

454	   Because the SCT is delivered in-band with the Certificate, neither
455	   the AS nor the VS perform any network round-trips to Certificate
456	   Transparency (CT) logs on the call path.

458	7.2.1.  Authentication Service Processing

460	   1.  Optional local SCT validation - For each embedded SCT the AS MAY:

462	       *  compute the hash of the TBSCertificate as defined in
463	          Section 3.2 of [RFC6962] and verify the SCT signature with the
464	          cached public key of the issuing CT log;

466	       *  verify that now() >= SCT.timestamp advertised by that log.

468	       *  Implementations SHOULD pre-compute and cache these checks at
469	          certificate activation time so that per-call signing incurs
470	          only an O(1) lookup.

472	   2.  PASSporT construction - The PASSporT header and payload are
473	       produced per [RFC8224] using the Certificate’s private key; the
474	       Certificate (with its SCT list) is conveyed in the 'x5u' or 'x5c'
475	       parameter.

477	   3.  Optional Failure handling - If no SCT validates, the AS MAY treat
478	       the Certificate as unusable and refuse to sign the call.

480	7.2.2.  Verification Service Processing

482	   Upon receipt of a SIP INVITE bearing an Identity header, the VS
483	   performs the steps below, all of which are deliberately offline to
484	   avoid adding call-path latency:

486	   1.  Verify PASSporT signature with DC public key.

488	   2.  Validate DC chain to an accepted STI trust anchor.

490	   3.  For each embedded SCT:

492	       *  Verify signature against cached log key.

494	       *  Ensure now() >= SCT.timestamp *or* defer to asynchronous
495	          auditor.

497	   Implementations SHOULD cache certificates and validated SCT objects
498	   for the lifetime of the certificates' notAfter field to amortize step
499	   3 across many calls.

501	7.2.3.  Performance and Scalability Guidelines

503	   *  No synchronous log queries - Embedded SCTs guarantee log
504	      commitment; therefore, AS/VS MUST NOT fetch proofs on the call
505	      path.

507	   *  Key caching - Log public keys are static and should be loaded at
508	      process start-up; reload only on key-roll events signaled via CT
509	      or operator policy.

511	7.3.  Monitor

513	   Monitors in the STI-CT framework play a crucial role in maintaining
514	   the integrity and trust of the ecosystem.  They ensure that no
515	   certificates are mis-issued, particularly concerning the TNAuthList
516	   field, which lists the telephone numbers an entity is authorized to
517	   use.

519	7.3.1.  Monitor Workflow

521	   1.  Initialize Monitor: Set up the Monitor to periodically query the
522	       transparency logs for new entries.  The Monitor must be
523	       configured with the base URL of each log it intends to monitor.
524	       Configure the Monitor with a list of telephone numbers (TNs) and/
525	       or associated SPC represented entities to track.

527	   2.  Retrieve Latest STH: The Monitor retrieves the latest Signed Tree
528	       Head (STH) from each log to determine the current state of the
529	       log.

531	       API Call: GET https://<log server>/stict/v1/get-sth

533	   3.  Retrieve New Entries from Log: Using the STH, the Monitor
534	       retrieves new entries from the log that have been added since the
535	       last known state.

537	       API Call: GET https://<log server>/stict/v1/get-
538	       entries?start=last_known_index&end=current_sth_index

540	   4.  Decode and Verify Certificates: Decode each retrieved certificate
541	       and verify its validity using the provided certificate chain.
542	       Extract the entity name and TNAuthList from the certificate.

544	   5.  Check for Mis-issuance: Compare the TNAuthList and entity name
545	       from the newly issued certificate with the Monitor's configured
546	       list.  Alarm if a certificate is issued in the name of a
547	       different entity for the same TNs.

549	   6.  Alarm and Reporting: If a mis-issuance is detected, raise an
550	       alarm and log the details for further investigation.  Notify
551	       relevant stakeholders to rectify any confirmed mis-issuance.

553	   7.  Maintain State and Continuity: Update the Monitor's last known
554	       state with the current STH index to ensure continuity in
555	       monitoring.

557	   8.  STH Verification and Consistency Check: After retrieving a new
558	       STH, verify the STH signature.  If not keeping all log entries,
559	       fetch a consistency proof for the new STH with the previous STH
560	       (GET https://<log server>/stict/v1/get-sth-consistency) and
561	       verify it.  Go to Step 5 and repeat the process.

563	7.4.  Auditor

565	   Auditors are responsible for verifying the consistency and
566	   correctness of the log, ensuring that the log behaves according to
567	   the expected protocol.  Auditors can operate as standalone services
568	   or as part of another client role, such as a monitor or an VS.

570	7.4.1.  Auditor Functions

572	   1.  STH Verification: Auditors can fetch STHs periodically and verify
573	       their signatures to ensure the log is maintaining its integrity.

575	       API Call: GET https://<log server>/stict/v1/get-sth

577	   2.  Consistency Proof Verification: Auditors verify the consistency
578	       of a log over time by requesting a consistency proof between two
579	       STHs.

581	       API Call: GET https://<log server>/stict/v1/get-sth-consistency

583	   3.  Audit Proof Verification: A certificate accompanied by an SCT can
584	       be verified against any STH dated after the SCT timestamp + the
585	       Maximum Merge Delay by requesting a Merkle audit proof.

587	       API Call: GET https://<log server>/stict/v1/get-proof-by-hash

589	   4.  Cross-Checking Logs: Auditors can cross-check entries across
590	       different logs by comparing SCTs and verifying that entries are
591	       consistently logged across the ecosystem.

593	   5.  Error and Inconsistency Detection: Any discrepancies or failures
594	       in verification processes can be logged as evidence of potential
595	       log misbehavior, and appropriate actions can be taken based on
596	       the findings.

598	8.  Relationship to RFC6962

600	   This document profiles the Certificate Transparency (CT) protocol as
601	   defined in [RFC6962] for use within the STIR eco-system.  All log
602	   data structures (e.g., LogEntry, SignedCertificateTimestamp,
603	   TreeHeadSignature) and API endpoints (e.g., add-pre-chain, get-sth,
604	   get-entries, etc.) are adopted directly from [RFC6962].

606	   The main differences are: - The expected certificate types are STI
607	   certificates as defined in [RFC8226] and [RFC9060], with TNAuthList
608	   extensions. - Submitters are limited to STI Certification Authorities
609	   and Subordinate Certification Authorities. - Monitoring and auditing
610	   are focused on detection of mis-issued telephone number or service
611	   provider codes (SPCs). - The client roles (e.g., VS, AS) interact
612	   with certificates and logs in ways specific to SIP call
613	   authentication.

615	9.  Security Considerations

617	   As this specification follows the guidance of [RFC6962], it also
618	   inherits the security considerations defined therein.

620	   The use of Certificate Transparency (CT) within the STIR ecosystem
621	   enhances accountability but also introduces operational and security
622	   risks that must be managed.  Trust in the system depends on the
623	   integrity of CT logs and therefore, logs should be operated by
624	   reputable entities and monitored to detect mis-issuance or
625	   inconsistent Signed Tree Heads (STHs).

627	   Signed Certificate Timestamps (SCTs) should be verified by
628	   Authentication Services (AS) before use for creating digital
629	   signatures and by Verification Services (VS) using trusted log public
630	   keys to prevent forgery or replay.  Key rotation and distribution
631	   should be performed securely.

633	   While CT does not prevent mis-issuance, it enables timely detection
634	   and monitors should track Telephone Number (TN) and Service Provider
635	   Code (SPC) associations and alert stakeholders if certificates are
636	   issued outside of authorized ranges.

638	   Because transparency may expose sensitive relationships between TNs
639	   and entities, implementers should consider privacy implications and
640	   minimize or redact logged data where appropriate.

642	   CT logs and Certification Authorities (CAs) should implement
643	   safeguards such as rate limiting, caching, and redundant storage to
644	   mitigate denial-of-service risks and ensure availability.
