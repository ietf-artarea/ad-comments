13	     Use of Remote Attestation with Certification Signing Requests
14	                  draft-ietf-lamps-csr-attestation-32

16	Abstract

18	   Certification Authorities (CAs) issuing certificates to Public Key
19	   Infrastructure (PKI) end entities may require a certificate signing
20	   request (CSR) to include additional verifiable information to confirm
21	   policy compliance.  For example, a CA may require an end entity to
22	   demonstrate that the private key corresponding to a CSR's public key
23	   is secured by a hardware security module (HSM), is not exportable,
24	   etc.  The process of generating, transmitting, and verifying
25	   additional information required by the CA is called remote
26	   attestation.  While work is currently underway to standardize various
27	   aspects of remote attestation, a variety of proprietary mechanisms
28	   have been in use for years, particularly regarding protection of
29	   private keys.

31	   This specification defines ASN.1 structures which may carry
32	   attestation data for PKCS#10 and Certificate Request Message Format
33	   (CRMF) messages.  Both standardized and proprietary attestation
34	   formats are supported by this specification.

107	1.  Introduction

109	   Certification Authorities (CAs) issuing certificates to PKI end
110	   entities may require that a certificate signing request (CSR) include
111	   verifiable attestations.  These attestations can contain claims
112	   regarding the platform used by the end entity to generate the key
113	   pair for which a certificate is sought.  These claims can also
114	   include attributes of the key pair with respect to its protection,
115	   use, and extractability.  At the time of writing, the most pressing
116	   example of the need for remote attestation in certificate enrollment
117	   is the Code-Signing Baseline Requirements (CSBR) document maintained
118	   by the CA/Browser Forum [CSBR].  The [CSBR] requires compliant CAs to
119	   "ensure that a Subscriber's Private Key is generated, stored, and
120	   used in a secure environment that has controls to prevent theft or
121	   misuse".  This requirement is a natural fit to enforce via remote
122	   attestation.

124	   This specification defines an attribute and an extension that allow
125	   for conveyance of verifiable attestations in several Certificate
126	   Signing Request (CSR) formats, including PKCS#10 [RFC2986] or
127	   Certificate Request Message Format (CRMF) [RFC4211] messages.  Given
128	   several standard and proprietary remote attestation technologies are
129	   in use, this specification is intended to be as technology-agnostic
130	   as is feasible with respect to implemented and future remote
131	   attestation technologies.  This aligns with the fact that a CA may
132	   wish to provide support for a variety of types of devices but cannot
133	   dictate what format a device uses to represent attestations.
134	   However, if a certificate requester does not include the number and
135	   types of attestations required by the CA, it is unlikely the
136	   requester will receive the requested certificate.

138	   While CSRs are defined using Abstract Syntax Notation One (ASN.1),
139	   attestations may be defined using any data description language,
140	   i.e., ASN.1 or Concise Data Description Language (CDDL), or
141	   represented using any type of encoding, including Distinguished
142	   Encoding Rules (DER), Concise Binary Object Representation (CBOR),
143	   JavaScript Object Notation (JSON).  This specification RECOMMENDS
144	   that attestations that are not encoded using the Basic Encoding Rules
145	   (BER) or Distinguished Encoding Rules (DER) be wrapped in an ASN.1
146	   OCTET STRING.

148	2.  Relationship to the IETF RATS Working Group

150	   As noted, attestation-related technologies have existed for many
151	   years, albeit with no standard format and no standard means of
152	   conveying attestation statements to a CA.  This draft addresses the
153	   latter, and is equally applicable to standard and proprietary
154	   attestation formats.  The IETF Remote Attestation Procedures (RATS)
155	   working group is addressing the former.  In [RFC9334], RATS defined
156	   vocabulary, architecture, and usage patterns related to the practice
157	   of generating and verifying attestations.

159	   In its simplest topological model, attestations are generated by the
160	   certificate requester and verified by the CA/RA.  Section 5 of
161	   [RFC9334] defines topological patterns that are more complex,
162	   including the background check model and the passport model.  This
163	   document may be applied to instantiating any of these topological
164	   models for CSR processing, provided the required security
165	   requirements specific to the context of certificate issuance are
166	   satisfied.

168	   Section 4.2 of [RFC9334] defines several roles that originate,
169	   forward or process attestation statements (also see Section 1.2 of
170	   [RFC9683]): the Attester, Endorser, Relying Party, and Verifier.
171	   Attestation statements, such as Evidence, may be directed to an
172	   entity taking at least one of these roles, including to an CA/RA
173	   acting as a Verifier.  An CA/RA may also forward attestation
174	   statements to a Verifier for appraisal.  Each attestation statements
175	   may contain one or more claims, including claims that may be required
176	   by an RA or CA.  Attestation statements transmitted by these parties
177	   are defined in Section 8 of [RFC9334] as the "conceptual messages"
178	   Evidence, Endorsement, and Attestation Results.  The structure
179	   defined in this specification may be used by any of the roles that
180	   originate attestation statements, and is equally applicable to these
181	   three conceptual messages.

183	3.  Conventions and Definitions

185	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
186	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
187	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
188	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
189	   capitals, as shown here.

191	   This document re-uses the terms defined in [RFC9334] related to
192	   remote attestation.  Readers of this document are assumed to be
193	   familiar with the following terms defined in [RFC9334]: Evidence,
194	   Endorsement, Claim, Attestation Result (AR), Attester, Relying Party,
195	   and Verifier.  Per [RFC9334], the CA/RA is the Relying Party with
196	   respect to remote attestation.  This use of the term "relying party"
197	   differs from the traditional PKIX use of the term.  This
198	   specification uses CA/RA to refer to an [RFC9334] Relying Party,
199	   which may or may not include an integrated Verifier.

201	   The term "Certification Request" message is defined in [RFC2986].
202	   Specifications, such as [RFC7030], later introduced the term
203	   "Certificate Signing Request (CSR)" to refer to the Certification
204	   Request message.  While the term "Certification Request" would have
205	   been correct, the mistake was unnoticed.  In the meanwhile CSR is an
206	   abbreviation used beyond PKCS#10.  Hence, it is equally applicable to
207	   other protocols that use a different syntax and even a different
208	   encoding, in particular this document also considers messages in the
209	   Certificate Request Message Format (CRMF) [RFC4211] to be "CSRs".  In
210	   this document, the terms "CSR" and Certificate Request message are
211	   used interchangeably.

213	   The term "hardware security module (HSM)" is used generically to
214	   refer to the combination of hardware and software designed to protect
215	   keys from unauthorized access.  Other commonly used terms include
216	   Secure Element, Trusted Platform Module, and Trusted Execution
217	   Environment.

219	   Since this document combines terminology from two domains, Remote
220	   Attestation (RATS) and X.509 PKI, it follows a naming convention to
221	   avoid ambiguity.  RATS terminology is written in uppercase (e.g.,
222	   Verifier), while X.509/PKI terminology is written in lowercase (e.g.,
223	   certification authority (CA)).  This distinction clarifies terms that
224	   exist in both domains; for instance, a Verifier refers to the RATS
225	   entity that processes Evidence, whereas a verifier refers to the PKI
226	   entity that validates certificates.  This convention is distinct from
227	   camel-case identifiers like "AttestationStatement", which denote
228	   ASN.1 types.

230	4.  Conveying Attestations in CSRs

232	   The focus of this specification is the conveyance of attestations to
233	   a CA/RA as part of a CSR.  The following sub-sections define formats
234	   to support this conveyance, an optional mechanism to limit support to
235	   specific attestation types at the ASN.1 level, and bindings to the
236	   attribute and extension mechanisms used in certificate managment
237	   protocols.

239	4.1.  AttestationStatement and AttestationBundle

241	   The AttestationStatement structure (as shown in Figure 1) facilitates
242	   the representation of Evidence, Endorsements, and Attestation Results
243	   generated by an Attester, Endorser, or Verifier for processing by a
244	   Verifier or Relying Party, such as verification by a CA/RA.

246	   *  The type field is an OBJECT IDENTIFIER that identifies the format
247	      of the stmt field.

249	   *  The stmt field contains the attestation for processing,
250	      constrained by the type field.  Formats that are not defined using
251	      ASN.1 MUST define an ASN.1 wrapper for use with the
252	      AttestationStatement structure.  For example, a CBOR-encoded
253	      format may be defined as an OCTET STRING for AttestationStatement
254	      purposes, where the contents of the OCTET STRING are the CBOR-
255	      encoded data.

257	   ATTESTATION-STATEMENT ::= TYPE-IDENTIFIER

259	   AttestationStatement ::= SEQUENCE {
260	      type   ATTESTATION-STATEMENT.&id({AttestationStatementSet}),
261	      stmt   ATTESTATION-STATEMENT.&Type(
262	                 {AttestationStatementSet}{@type})
263	   }

265	                Figure 1: Definition of AttestationStatement

267	   In some cases, a CA may require CSRs to include a variety of claims,
268	   which may require the cooperation of more than one Attester.
269	   Similarly, a CA/RA may outsource verification of claims from
270	   different Attesters to a single Verifier.  The AttestationBundle
271	   structure, Figure 2, facilitates the representation of one or more
272	   AttestationStatement structures along with an OPTIONAL collection of
273	   certificates that may be useful for certification path building and
274	   validation to verify each AttestationStatement.  AttestationBundle is
275	   the structure included in a CSR attribute or extension.

277	   AttestationBundle ::= SEQUENCE {
278	      attestations SEQUENCE SIZE (1..MAX) OF AttestationStatement,
279	      certs SEQUENCE SIZE (1..MAX) OF LimitedCertChoices OPTIONAL,
280	   }

282	                 Figure 2: Definition of AttestationBundle

284	   A CA/RA's issuance policy may place additional requirements on the
285	   contents of the attestations sequence, such as requiring claims that
286	   are bound to the CSR's public key in a cryptographically verifiable
287	   manner.  Such a binding may identify the public key directly or
288	   through a cryptographically verifiable reference, as defined by the
289	   applicable attestation format.  See Section 6 for more discussion.

291	   The CertificateChoices structure defined in [RFC6268], and reproduced
292	   below along with OtherCertificateFormat, allows for carrying
293	   certificates in the default X.509 [RFC5280] format or in other
294	   certificate formats.  Within an AttestationBundle, a
295	   CertificateChoices value MUST use only the certificate or other
296	   alternative; the extendedCertificate, v1AttrCert, and v2AttrCert
297	   alternatives MUST NOT be used.  [RFC6268] marks extendedCertificate
298	   and v1AttrCert as obsolete.  Although v2AttrCert is not obsolete, it
299	   is excluded here because an attribute certificate does not contain a
300	   subject public key that can be used to validate an
301	   AttestationStatement.

303	   OTHER-CERT-FMT is an ASN.1 information object class.  The &id field
304	   identifies a certificate format by its OBJECT IDENTIFIER, and the
305	   corresponding &Type field specifies the ASN.1 type used to carry that
306	   format.  For a non-ASN.1 certificate format, an OTHER-CERT-FMT
307	   information object MUST specify OCTET STRING as its &Type;
308	   otherCertFormat then carries the format's &id, and otherCert carries
309	   the encoded certificate in that OCTET STRING.  LimitedCertChoices
310	   applies an ASN.1 constraint that limits the available alternatives to
311	   certificate and other.

313	      CertificateChoices ::= CHOICE {
314	        certificate Certificate,
315	        extendedCertificate [0] IMPLICIT ExtendedCertificate,
316	             -- Obsolete
317	        ...,
318	        [[3: v1AttrCert [1] IMPLICIT AttributeCertificateV1]],
319	             -- Obsolete
320	        [[4: v2AttrCert [2] IMPLICIT AttributeCertificateV2]],
321	        [[5: other      [3] IMPLICIT OtherCertificateFormat]] }

323	      OTHER-CERT-FMT ::= TYPE-IDENTIFIER

325	      OtherCertificateFormat ::= SEQUENCE {
326	        otherCertFormat OTHER-CERT-FMT.&id({SupportedCertFormats}),
327	        otherCert       OTHER-CERT-FMT.&Type(
328	                {SupportedCertFormats}{@otherCertFormat})}

330	      LimitedCertChoices ::=
331	         CertificateChoices
332	             (WITH COMPONENTS {certificate, other})

334	   The certs field is OPTIONAL.  When validation of an
335	   AttestationStatement requires a public key that cannot be obtained
336	   otherwise, certs SHOULD contain a certificate containing that public
337	   key.  A certificate is not needed when the corresponding public key
338	   is already known to the Verifier or is embedded within the
339	   AttestationStatement.  Additional certificates MAY be provided, for
340	   example, to chain the attestation key back to a trust anchor.  No
341	   specific order of the certificates in certs should be expected
342	   because certificates contained in certs may be needed to validate
343	   different AttestationStatement instances.

345	   This specification places no restriction on mixing certificate types
346	   within the certs field.  For example a non-X.509 attestation signer
347	   certificate MAY chain to a trust anchor via a chain of X.509
348	   certificates.  It is up to the Attester and its Verifier to agree on
349	   supported certificate formats.

351	4.2.  AttestationStatementSet

353	   AttestationStatementSet ATTESTATION-STATEMENT ::= {
354	      ... -- None defined in this document --
355	   }

357	              Figure 3: Definition of AttestationStatementSet

359	   The expression illustrated in Figure 3 maps ASN.1 Types for
360	   attestation statements to the OIDs that identify them.  These
361	   mappings are used to construct or parse AttestationStatement objects
362	   that appear in an AttestationBundle.  Attestation statements are
363	   typically defined in other IETF standards, in standards produced by

365	   corresponding OIDs that identify them.  AttestationStatementSet is
366	   left unconstrained in this document.  However, implementers MAY
367	   populate it with the formats that they wish to support.  This
368	   document does not define, allocate, or register an OID for any

370	   for such formats.  Specification authors who define an attestation-

372	   under their control.

374	4.3.  CSR Attribute and Extension

376	   The ASN.1 ATTRIBUTE and EXTENSION information object classes are
377	   defined in [RFC5912].  Attributes within a PKCS#10 CSR use ATTRIBUTE,
378	   and extensions within a CRMF CSR use EXTENSION.

380	   id-aa-attestation OBJECT IDENTIFIER ::= { id-aa 59 }

382	   -- For PKCS#10
383	   attr-attestations ATTRIBUTE ::= {
384	     TYPE AttestationBundle
385	     COUNTS MAX 1
386	     IDENTIFIED BY id-aa-attestation
387	   }

389	   -- For CRMF
390	   ext-attestations EXTENSION ::= {
391	     SYNTAX AttestationBundle
392	     IDENTIFIED BY id-aa-attestation
393	   }

395	            Figure 4: Definitions of CSR attribute and extension

397	   The Extension variant illustrated in Figure 4 is intended only for
398	   use within CRMF CSRs and is NOT RECOMMENDED to be used within X.509
399	   certificates due to the privacy implications of publishing
400	   information about the end entity's hardware environment.

402	   Multiple different types of AttestationStatement(s) may be included
403	   within a single top-level AttestationBundle.  Note that this document
404	   does not require the AttestationBundle.attestations field to contain
405	   only one AttestationStatement of a given type.  For example, if a
406	   given type is a "wrapper" type containing the conceptual message
407	   wrapper (CMW) structure [RFC9999], multiple copies of a CMW-typed
408	   AttestationStatement may be included.

410	   Per [RFC5280] no more than one instance of a given type of Extension
411	   may be carried within an Extensions structure, so an Extensions
412	   structure MUST contain no more than one Extension of type id-aa-
413	   attestation.

415	   PKCS#10 uses the legacy structures Attributes and Attribute rather
416	   than the later defined SingleAttribute and AttributeSet structures -
417	   all of which are defined against the ATTRIBUTE ASN.1 CLASS.  The
418	   ATTRIBUTE CLASS has a COUNTS MAX n clause which can be used to limit
419	   the copies of ATTRIBUTE related structures.  For the purposes of this
420	   document the COUNTS MAX 1 clause in the attr-attestation shall be
421	   taken to mean the following:

423	   *  An Attributes structure carried within a PKCS#10 CSR MUST contain
424	      no more than one Attribute of type id-aa-attestation.

426	   *  An Attribute of type id-aa-attestation MUST contain exactly one
427	      copy of an AttestationBundle.

429	   When multiple Verifiers support the same attestation-format OID,
430	   ambiguity can arise in routing attestations to the appropriate
431	   Verifier.  Resolving that ambiguity is outside the scope of this
432	   document and must be defined by the attestation-format specification,
433	   particularly for opaque (wrapper) formats.  Two pragmatic approaches
434	   are recommended: (1) assign distinct OIDs for different verifier or
435	   verification types even when the underlying format structure is
436	   identical, or (2) encapsulate the opaque attestation object in a
437	   wrapper that carries an explicit hint.  Implementations should adopt
438	   one of these approaches and attestation-format specifications should
439	   mandate the precise mechanism for nonce selection and routing of
440	   attestations.

477	6.  Security Considerations

479	   This document defines a structure to convey attestations as
480	   additional information in CSRs, as well as an attribute to convey
481	   that structure in the Certification Request Message defined in
482	   [RFC2986] and an extension to convey that structure in the
483	   Certificate Request Message Format defined in [RFC4211].  The CA/RA
484	   that receives the CSR may choose to verify the attestation(s) to
485	   determine if an issuance policy is met, or which of a suite of
486	   policies is satisfied.  The CA/RA is also free to discard the
487	   additional information without processing.

489	   A CA which accepts or requires attestation(s) SHOULD document its
490	   requirements with its Certification Practice Statement(s) [RFC3647].

492	   The remainder of this section identifies security considerations that
493	   apply when the CA/RA chooses to verify the attestation as part of the
494	   evaluation of a CSR.

496	6.1.  Binding Attestations to the CSR's Public Key

498	   Key attestation is one use case for the structures defined in this
499	   document.  It enables a CA/RA to obtain assurance about the key pair
500	   for which a certificate is requested, including the generation,
501	   protection, permitted use, or extractability of its private key.
502	   Platform attestation may provide additional claims about the
503	   environment in which the key pair is generated, stored, or used.  To
504	   provide assurance about the requested key pair, the relevant claims
505	   must be bound to the CSR's public key in a cryptographically
506	   verifiable manner, and appraisal must establish that they support the
507	   claimed properties of that key pair.  Including a requester-supplied
508	   public key or its hash in a signed platform attestation does not, by
509	   itself, demonstrate that the corresponding private key was generated
510	   or is protected in the attested environment.

512	   Regardless of the topological model, when a CA/RA relies on
513	   attestation statements to establish a property of the CSR's public
514	   key or the corresponding private key, it MUST ensure that this
515	   binding has been validated.  The CA/RA may rely on a trusted Verifier
516	   to perform this validation but remains responsible for ensuring that
517	   it has been performed.  The binding may identify the public key
518	   directly or through a cryptographically verifiable reference, as
519	   defined by the applicable attestation format.

521	   When Evidence is used to establish such a key property, validation
522	   includes establishing that the claims refer to the requested key pair
523	   and support the properties on which the CA/RA relies.  This appraisal
524	   may be performed by a CA/RA acting as a Verifier or by an external
525	   trusted Verifier.  When Attestation Results are used for this
526	   purpose, the CA/RA MUST authenticate the results and establish that
527	   they apply to the CSR's public key and confirm the required
528	   properties based on a binding validated by the trusted Verifier.
529	   These requirements apply whether Evidence or Attestation Results are
530	   carried in the CSR or obtained during its processing.  For the code-
531	   signing use case described in Section 1, the CA/RA therefore needs
532	   assurance that the claims of HSM generation and protection apply to
533	   the key pair for which the certificate is requested.

535	   If no validated binding is available, the CA/RA MUST NOT treat
536	   attestation statements as establishing properties of the CSR's public
537	   key or the corresponding private key.  The CA/RA's issuance policy
538	   determines whether to reject the CSR, obtain additional evidence, or
539	   issue a certificate based on other acceptable information.  The
540	   absence of a validated binding does not, by itself, require rejection
541	   under this specification.  However, an issuance policy that requires
542	   attested properties of the requested key cannot be satisfied by
543	   attestation statements that lack such validated binding.

545	   Multiple attestation statements from multiple sources, as envisioned
546	   in [RFC9334], can introduce additional complications as shown in the
547	   following example.

549	   For example, a CA may have an issuance policy that requires key
550	   generation in an HSM on a company-owned platform in a known good
551	   state.  The CSR might contain three AttestationStatements originated
552	   by three different attesters:

554	   1.  that a key pair was generated in an HSM;

556	   2.  that a particular platform is company-owned; and

558	   3.  that a particular platform was in a known good state (e.g, up to
559	       date on patches, etc.).

561	   While each of these attestations may be independently correct, the
562	   CA/RA is responsible for confirming the attestations apply in concert
563	   to the public key in the CSR.  That is, the CA/RA must analyze the
564	   attestations to ensure that:

566	   1.  the attestation of HSM generation by AttestationStatement 1
567	       applies to the public key in the CSR;

569	   2.  the attestation of company ownership by AttestationStatement 2
570	       applies to the platform that contains the HSM; and

572	   3.  the attestation that a platform was in a known good state by
573	       AttestationStatement 3 applies to the platform that contains the
574	       HSM.

576	6.2.  Freshness

578	   To avoid replay attacks, the CA/RA may choose to ignore attestation
579	   statements that are stale, or whose freshness cannot be determined.
580	   Mechanisms to address freshness and their application to the RATS
581	   topological models are discussed in [RFC9334].  Other mechanisms for
582	   determining freshness may be used as the CA/RA deems appropriate.
583	   When CSRs are embedded within certificate management protocols such
584	   as Certificate Management Protocol (CMP) [RFC9810], Enrollment over
585	   Secure Transport (EST) [RFC7030], and Certificate Management over CMS
586	   (CMC) [RFC10002], these protocols can supply an Attester with a
587	   nonce, for example.  Further details are specified in
588	   [I-D.ietf-lamps-attestation-freshness].

590	6.3.  Resource Exhaustion

592	   CSRs are untrusted input to a CA/RA, and processing attestation
593	   bundles may consume substantial resources through parsing,
594	   cryptographic verification, certification path building and
595	   validation, and requests to external Verifiers or other services.  An
596	   attacker can exploit these costs to cause resource exhaustion, even
597	   with a syntactically valid bundle.

599	   Implementations SHOULD impose limits appropriate to their deployment
600	   on the total bundle size, the number, type and size of attestation
601	   statements and certificates, parsing depth, and the computational
602	   effort spent processing a request.  These limits SHOULD also bound
603	   certification path building, as well as the number, duration, and
604	   concurrency of external requests.  Implementations SHOULD enforce
605	   limits as early as practicable.  When a limit is exceeded,
606	   implementations SHOULD stop the affected processing and release
607	   resources allocated to it.  An attestation statement whose
608	   verification has not completed successfully MUST NOT be treated as
609	   successfully verified.

611	6.4.  Additional Security Considerations

613	   In addition to the security considerations listed here, implementers
614	   should be familiar with the security considerations of the
615	   specifications on which this specification depends: PKCS#10
616	   [RFC2986], CRMF [RFC4211], as well as general security concepts
617	   relating to remote attestation.  Many of these concepts are discussed
618	   in Section 6 of [RFC9334], Section 7 of [RFC9334], Section 9 of
619	   [RFC9334], Section 11 of [RFC9334], and Section 12 of [RFC9334].

621	   Implementers should also be aware of any security considerations
622	   relating to the specific attestation formats being carried within the
623	   CSR.

625	7.  Privacy Considerations

627	   Attestation statements and accompanying certificates can disclose
628	   information about a device, its software and hardware environment,
629	   and its owner or users.  These statements may contain Evidence,
630	   Endorsements, or Attestation Results.  Attesters SHOULD consider how
631	   much information is appropriate to disclose to a given CA.  Examples
632	   include hardware identifiers, software versions, patch levels, and
633	   device ownership.  These disclosures occur during enrollment, even if
634	   no attestation data is copied into the issued certificate.

636	   Attestation formats may use stable device-identifying public keys,
637	   certificates, device IDs, or other identifiers.  These values can
638	   enable a CA/RA or Verifier to link multiple CSRs to the same device
639	   even when the CSR public key, subject name, or enrollment account
640	   changes.  Such linkability can affect privacy even when the
641	   individual attested claims are not otherwise sensitive.

643	   A CA/RA that forwards attestation statements or accompanying
644	   certificates to an external Verifier discloses this information to
645	   another party.  That party may also be able to correlate requests
646	   across enrollments or across CAs.  The privacy assessment of a
647	   deployment therefore needs to cover the entire enrollment and
648	   attestation verification path, including third-party processing,
649	   retention, and logging.

651	   Certificate requesters, CAs/RAs, and Verifiers SHOULD minimize the
652	   claims contained in attestation statements and the accompanying
653	   certificates they disclose, process, and retain to those needed for
654	   the applicable issuance policy.  Where supported by the attestation
655	   technology and compatible with that policy, deployments SHOULD use
656	   mechanisms that reduce device linkability, such as privacy-preserving
657	   attestation procedures.  Such mechanisms do not eliminate correlation
658	   through other disclosed information.  Enrollment protocols and
659	   communication with external Verifiers SHOULD provide confidentiality
660	   protection and authenticate recipients.  Transport confidentiality
661	   protects against observers but does not prevent correlation by the
662	   recipients themselves.  Access to stored attestation statements and
663	   accompanying certificates, including copies in logs, SHOULD be
664	   restricted, and retention SHOULD be limited to the period needed for
665	   issuance and applicable audit requirements.

667	   Attestation statements are intended as additional information in the
668	   issuance process.  It is NOT RECOMMENDED for a CA to copy attestation
669	   statements into the published certificate.  CAs that choose to
670	   republish attestation statements or claims contained in them in
671	   certificates SHOULD review the contents and omit sensitive or
672	   identifying information.

674	   Further discussion of remote attestation privacy risks and
675	   mitigations is provided in Section 11 of [RFC9334].  Deployments
676	   should also consider the privacy considerations of the specific
677	   attestation formats they use.
