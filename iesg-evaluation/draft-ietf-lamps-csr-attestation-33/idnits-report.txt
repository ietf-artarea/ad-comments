13	     Use of Remote Attestation with Certification Signing Requests
14	                  draft-ietf-lamps-csr-attestation-33

16	Abstract

18	   Certification Authorities (CAs) issuing certificates to Public Key
19	   Infrastructure (PKI) end entities may require a certificate signing
20	   request (CSR) to include additional verifiable information to confirm
21	   policy compliance.  For example, a CA may require an end entity to
22	   demonstrate that the private key corresponding to a CSR's public key
23	   is secured by a hardware security module (HSM), is not exportable,
24	   etc.  The process of generating, transmitting, and verifying
25	   additional information required by the CA is called remote
26	   attestation.  While work is currently underway to standardize various
27	   aspects of remote attestation, a variety of proprietary mechanisms
28	   have been in use for years, particularly regarding protection of
29	   private keys.

31	   This specification defines ASN.1 structures which may carry
32	   attestation data for PKCS#10 and Certificate Request Message Format
33	   (CRMF) messages.  Both standardized and proprietary attestation
34	   formats are supported by this specification.

107	1.  Introduction

109	   Certification Authorities (CAs) issuing certificates to PKI end
110	   entities may require that a certificate signing request (CSR) include
111	   verifiable attestations.  These attestations can contain claims
112	   regarding the platform used by the end entity to generate the key
113	   pair for which a certificate is sought.  These claims can also
114	   include attributes of the key pair with respect to its protection,
115	   use, and extractability.  At the time of writing, the most pressing
116	   example of the need for remote attestation in certificate enrollment
117	   is the Code-Signing Baseline Requirements (CSBR) document maintained
118	   by the CA/Browser Forum [CSBR].  The [CSBR] requires compliant CAs to
119	   "ensure that a Subscriber's Private Key is generated, stored, and
120	   used in a secure environment that has controls to prevent theft or
121	   misuse".  This requirement is a natural fit to enforce via remote
122	   attestation.

124	   This specification defines an attribute and an extension that allow
125	   for conveyance of verifiable attestations in several Certificate
126	   Signing Request (CSR) formats, including PKCS#10 [RFC2986] or
127	   Certificate Request Message Format (CRMF) [RFC4211] messages.  Given
128	   several standard and proprietary remote attestation technologies are
129	   in use, this specification is intended to be as technology-agnostic
130	   as is feasible with respect to implemented and future remote
131	   attestation technologies.  This aligns with the fact that a CA may
132	   wish to provide support for a variety of types of devices but cannot
133	   dictate what format a device uses to represent attestations.
134	   However, if a certificate requester does not include the number and
135	   types of attestations required by the CA, it is unlikely the
136	   requester will receive the requested certificate.

138	   While CSRs are defined using Abstract Syntax Notation One (ASN.1),
139	   attestations may be defined using any data description language,
140	   i.e., ASN.1 or Concise Data Description Language (CDDL), or
141	   represented using any type of encoding, including Distinguished
142	   Encoding Rules (DER), Concise Binary Object Representation (CBOR),
143	   JavaScript Object Notation (JSON).  The requirements for carrying
144	   attestation formats not defined using ASN.1 are specified in
145	   Section 4.1.

147	2.  Relationship to the IETF RATS Working Group

149	   As noted, attestation-related technologies have existed for many
150	   years, albeit with no standard format and no standard means of
151	   conveying attestation statements to a CA.  This draft addresses the
152	   latter, and is equally applicable to standard and proprietary
153	   attestation formats.  The IETF Remote Attestation Procedures (RATS)
154	   working group is addressing the former.  In [RFC9334], RATS defined
155	   vocabulary, architecture, and usage patterns related to the practice
156	   of generating and verifying attestations.

158	   In its simplest topological model, attestations are generated by the
159	   certificate requester and verified by the CA/RA.  Section 5 of
160	   [RFC9334] defines topological patterns that are more complex,
161	   including the background check model and the passport model.  This
162	   document may be applied to instantiating any of these topological
163	   models for CSR processing, provided the required security
164	   requirements specific to the context of certificate issuance are
165	   satisfied.

167	   Section 4.1 of [RFC9334] defines several roles that originate,
168	   forward or process attestation statements (also see Section 1.2 of
169	   [RFC9683]): the Attester, Endorser, Relying Party, and Verifier.
170	   Attestation statements, such as Evidence, may be directed to an
171	   entity taking at least one of these roles, including to an CA/RA
172	   acting as a Verifier.  An CA/RA may also forward attestation
173	   statements to a Verifier for appraisal.  Each attestation statements
174	   may contain one or more claims, including claims that may be required
175	   by an RA or CA.  Attestation statements transmitted by these parties
176	   are defined in Section 8 of [RFC9334] as the "conceptual messages"
177	   Evidence, Endorsement, and Attestation Results.  The structure
178	   defined in this specification may be used by any of the roles that
179	   originate attestation statements, and is equally applicable to these
180	   three conceptual messages.

182	3.  Conventions and Definitions

184	   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
185	   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
186	   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
187	   BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
188	   capitals, as shown here.

190	   This document re-uses the terms defined in [RFC9334] related to
191	   remote attestation.  Readers of this document are assumed to be
192	   familiar with the following terms defined in [RFC9334]: Evidence,
193	   Endorsement, Claim, Attestation Result (AR), Attester, Relying Party,
194	   and Verifier.  Per [RFC9334], the CA/RA is the Relying Party with
195	   respect to remote attestation.  This use of the term "relying party"
196	   differs from the traditional PKIX use of the term.  This
197	   specification uses CA/RA to refer to an [RFC9334] Relying Party,
198	   which may or may not include an integrated Verifier.

200	   The term "Certification Request" message is defined in [RFC2986].
201	   Specifications, such as [RFC7030], later introduced the term
202	   "Certificate Signing Request (CSR)" to refer to the Certification
203	   Request message.  While the term "Certification Request" would have
204	   been correct, the mistake was unnoticed.  In the meanwhile CSR is an
205	   abbreviation used beyond PKCS#10.  Hence, it is equally applicable to
206	   other protocols that use a different syntax and even a different
207	   encoding, in particular this document also considers messages in the
208	   Certificate Request Message Format (CRMF) [RFC4211] to be "CSRs".  In
209	   this document, the terms "CSR" and Certificate Request message are
210	   used interchangeably.

212	   The term "hardware security module (HSM)" is used generically to
213	   refer to the combination of hardware and software designed to protect
214	   keys from unauthorized access.  Other commonly used terms include
215	   Secure Element, Trusted Platform Module, and Trusted Execution
216	   Environment.

218	   Since this document combines terminology from two domains, Remote
219	   Attestation (RATS) and X.509 PKI, it follows a naming convention to
220	   avoid ambiguity.  RATS terminology is written in uppercase (e.g.,
221	   Verifier), while X.509/PKI terminology is written in lowercase (e.g.,
222	   certification authority (CA)).  This distinction clarifies terms that
223	   exist in both domains; for instance, a Verifier refers to the RATS
224	   entity that processes Evidence, whereas a verifier refers to the PKI
225	   entity that validates certificates.  This convention is distinct from
226	   camel-case identifiers like "AttestationStatement", which denote
227	   ASN.1 types.

229	4.  Conveying Attestations in CSRs

231	   The focus of this specification is the conveyance of attestations to
232	   a CA/RA as part of a CSR.  The following sub-sections define formats
233	   to support this conveyance, an optional mechanism to limit support to
234	   specific attestation types at the ASN.1 level, and bindings to the
235	   attribute and extension mechanisms used in certificate managment
236	   protocols.

238	   The ASN.1 notation, information object classes, and constraint
239	   notation used in this section and in Appendix B are specified in
240	   [X680], [X681], and [X682], respectively.  For additional background
241	   on information object classes and constraints used in ASN.1 modules,
242	   see Sections 2.1.3 and 2.2 of [RFC6025].

244	4.1.  AttestationStatement and AttestationBundle

246	   The AttestationStatement structure (as shown in Figure 1) facilitates
247	   the representation of Evidence, Endorsements, and Attestation Results
248	   generated by an Attester, Endorser, or Verifier for processing by a
249	   Verifier or Relying Party, such as verification by a CA/RA.

251	   *  The type field is an OBJECT IDENTIFIER that identifies the format
252	      of the stmt field.

254	   *  The stmt field contains the attestation for processing,
255	      constrained by the type field.  Formats that are not defined using
256	      ASN.1 MUST define an ASN.1 wrapper for use with the
257	      AttestationStatement structure.  For example, a CBOR-encoded
258	      format may be defined as an OCTET STRING for AttestationStatement
259	      purposes, where the contents of the OCTET STRING are the CBOR-
260	      encoded data.

262	   ATTESTATION-STATEMENT ::= TYPE-IDENTIFIER

264	   AttestationStatement ::= SEQUENCE {
265	      type   ATTESTATION-STATEMENT.&id({AttestationStatementSet}),
266	      stmt   ATTESTATION-STATEMENT.&Type(
267	                 {AttestationStatementSet}{@type})
268	   }

270	                Figure 1: Definition of AttestationStatement

272	   In some cases, a CA may require CSRs to include a variety of claims,
273	   which may require the cooperation of more than one Attester.
274	   Similarly, a CA/RA may outsource verification of claims from
275	   different Attesters to a single Verifier.  The AttestationBundle
276	   structure, Figure 2, facilitates the representation of one or more
277	   AttestationStatement structures along with an OPTIONAL collection of
278	   certificates that may be useful for certification path building and
279	   validation to verify each AttestationStatement.  AttestationBundle is
280	   the structure included in a CSR attribute or extension.

282	   AttestationBundle ::= SEQUENCE {
283	      attestations SEQUENCE SIZE (1..MAX) OF AttestationStatement,
284	      certs SEQUENCE SIZE (1..MAX) OF LimitedCertChoices OPTIONAL,
285	   }

287	                 Figure 2: Definition of AttestationBundle

289	   A CA/RA's issuance policy may place additional requirements on the
290	   contents of the attestations sequence, such as requiring claims that
291	   are bound to the CSR's public key in a cryptographically verifiable
292	   manner.  Such a binding may identify the public key directly or
293	   through a cryptographically verifiable reference, as defined by the
294	   applicable attestation format.  See Section 6 for more discussion.

296	   The CertificateChoices structure defined in [RFC6268], and reproduced
297	   below along with OtherCertificateFormat, allows for carrying
298	   certificates in the default X.509 [RFC5280] format or in other
299	   certificate formats.  Within an AttestationBundle, a
300	   CertificateChoices value MUST use only the certificate or other
301	   alternative; the extendedCertificate, v1AttrCert, and v2AttrCert
302	   alternatives MUST NOT be used.  [RFC6268] marks extendedCertificate
303	   and v1AttrCert as obsolete.  Although v2AttrCert is not obsolete, it
304	   is excluded here because an attribute certificate does not contain a
305	   subject public key that can be used to validate an
306	   AttestationStatement.

308	   OTHER-CERT-FMT is an ASN.1 information object class.  The &id field
309	   identifies a certificate format by its OBJECT IDENTIFIER, and the
310	   corresponding &Type field specifies the ASN.1 type used to carry that
311	   format.  For a non-ASN.1 certificate format, an OTHER-CERT-FMT
312	   information object MUST specify OCTET STRING as its &Type;
313	   otherCertFormat then carries the format's &id, and otherCert carries
314	   the encoded certificate in that OCTET STRING.  LimitedCertChoices
315	   applies an ASN.1 constraint that limits the available alternatives to
316	   certificate and other.

318	      CertificateChoices ::= CHOICE {
319	        certificate Certificate,
320	        extendedCertificate [0] IMPLICIT ExtendedCertificate,
321	             -- Obsolete
322	        ...,
323	        [[3: v1AttrCert [1] IMPLICIT AttributeCertificateV1]],
324	             -- Obsolete
325	        [[4: v2AttrCert [2] IMPLICIT AttributeCertificateV2]],
326	        [[5: other      [3] IMPLICIT OtherCertificateFormat]] }

328	      OTHER-CERT-FMT ::= TYPE-IDENTIFIER

330	      OtherCertificateFormat ::= SEQUENCE {
331	        otherCertFormat OTHER-CERT-FMT.&id({SupportedCertFormats}),
332	        otherCert       OTHER-CERT-FMT.&Type(
333	                {SupportedCertFormats}{@otherCertFormat})}

335	      LimitedCertChoices ::=
336	         CertificateChoices
337	             (WITH COMPONENTS {certificate, other})

339	   The certs field is OPTIONAL.  When validation of an
340	   AttestationStatement requires a public key that cannot be obtained
341	   otherwise, certs SHOULD contain a certificate containing that public
342	   key.  A certificate is not needed when the corresponding public key
343	   is already known to the Verifier or is embedded within the
344	   AttestationStatement.  Additional certificates MAY be provided, for
345	   example, to chain the attestation key back to a trust anchor.  No
346	   specific order of the certificates in certs should be expected
347	   because certificates contained in certs may be needed to validate
348	   different AttestationStatement instances.

350	   This specification places no restriction on mixing certificate types
351	   within the certs field.  For example a non-X.509 attestation signer
352	   certificate MAY chain to a trust anchor via a chain of X.509
353	   certificates.  It is up to the Attester and its Verifier to agree on
354	   supported certificate formats.

356	4.2.  AttestationStatementSet

358	   AttestationStatementSet ATTESTATION-STATEMENT ::= {
359	      ... -- None defined in this document --
360	   }

362	              Figure 3: Definition of AttestationStatementSet

364	   The expression illustrated in Figure 3 maps ASN.1 Types for
365	   attestation statements to the OIDs that identify them.  These
366	   mappings are used to construct or parse AttestationStatement objects
367	   that appear in an AttestationBundle.  Attestation statements are
368	   typically defined in other IETF standards, in standards produced by
369	   other standards bodies, or as vendor proprietary formats along with
370	   corresponding OIDs that identify them.  AttestationStatementSet is
371	   left unconstrained in this document.  However, implementers MAY
372	   populate it with the formats that they wish to support.  This
373	   document does not define, allocate, or register an OID for any
374	   attestation-statement format, nor does it establish an IANA registry
375	   for such formats.  Specification authors who define an attestation-
376	   statement format are responsible for assigning its OID from an arc
377	   under their control.

379	4.3.  CSR Attribute and Extension

381	   The ASN.1 ATTRIBUTE and EXTENSION information object classes are
382	   defined in [RFC5912].  Attributes within a PKCS#10 CSR use ATTRIBUTE,
383	   and extensions within a CRMF CSR use EXTENSION.  The id-aa object
384	   identifier is defined in the SecureMimeMessageV3dot1-2009 module in
385	   Section 5 of [RFC5911].

387	   id-aa-attestation OBJECT IDENTIFIER ::= { id-aa 59 }

389	   -- For PKCS#10
390	   attr-attestation ATTRIBUTE ::= {
391	     TYPE AttestationBundle
392	     COUNTS MAX 1
393	     IDENTIFIED BY id-aa-attestation
394	   }

396	   -- For CRMF
397	   ext-attestation EXTENSION ::= {
398	     SYNTAX AttestationBundle
399	     IDENTIFIED BY id-aa-attestation
400	   }

402	            Figure 4: Definitions of CSR attribute and extension

404	   The Extension variant illustrated in Figure 4 is intended only for
405	   use within CRMF CSRs and is NOT RECOMMENDED to be used within X.509
406	   certificates due to the privacy implications of publishing
407	   information about the end entity's hardware environment.

409	   Multiple different types of AttestationStatement(s) may be included
410	   within a single top-level AttestationBundle.  Note that this document
411	   does not require the AttestationBundle.attestations field to contain
412	   only one AttestationStatement of a given type.  For example, if a
413	   given type is a "wrapper" type containing the conceptual message
414	   wrapper (CMW) structure [RFC9999], multiple copies of a CMW-typed
415	   AttestationStatement may be included.

417	   Per [RFC5280] no more than one instance of a given type of Extension
418	   may be carried within an Extensions structure, so an Extensions
419	   structure MUST contain no more than one Extension of type id-aa-
420	   attestation.

422	   PKCS#10 uses the legacy structures Attributes and Attribute rather
423	   than the later defined SingleAttribute and AttributeSet structures -
424	   all of which are defined against the ATTRIBUTE ASN.1 CLASS.  The
425	   ATTRIBUTE CLASS has a COUNTS MAX n clause which can be used to limit
426	   the copies of ATTRIBUTE related structures.  For the purposes of this
427	   document the COUNTS MAX 1 clause in the attr-attestation shall be
428	   taken to mean the following:

430	   *  An Attributes structure carried within a PKCS#10 CSR MUST contain
431	      no more than one Attribute of type id-aa-attestation.

433	   *  An Attribute of type id-aa-attestation MUST contain exactly one
434	      copy of an AttestationBundle.

436	   When multiple Verifiers support the same attestation-format OID,
437	   ambiguity can arise in routing attestations to the appropriate
438	   Verifier.  The attestation-format specification is responsible for
439	   defining how this ambiguity is resolved, particularly for opaque
440	   (wrapper) formats.  Possible approaches include defining separate
441	   OIDs for different Verifier or verification types, even when they
442	   share a format structure, or defining a wrapper with a hint that
443	   assists Verifier selection.  This document does not specify a
444	   Verifier selection mechanism.

476	6.  Security Considerations

478	   This document defines a structure to convey attestations as
479	   additional information in CSRs, as well as an attribute to convey
480	   that structure in the Certification Request Message defined in
481	   [RFC2986] and an extension to convey that structure in the
482	   Certificate Request Message Format defined in [RFC4211].  The CA/RA
483	   that receives the CSR may choose to verify the attestation(s) to
484	   determine if an issuance policy is met, or which of a suite of
485	   policies is satisfied.  The CA/RA is also free to discard the
486	   additional information without processing.

488	   A CA which accepts or requires attestation(s) SHOULD document its
489	   requirements with its Certification Practice Statement(s) [RFC3647].

491	   The remainder of this section identifies security considerations that
492	   apply when the CA/RA chooses to verify the attestation as part of the
493	   evaluation of a CSR.

495	6.1.  Binding Attestations to the CSR's Public Key

497	   Key attestation is one use case for the structures defined in this
498	   document.  It enables a CA/RA to obtain assurance about the key pair
499	   for which a certificate is requested, including the generation,
500	   protection, permitted use, or extractability of its private key.
501	   Platform attestation may provide additional claims about the
502	   environment in which the key pair is generated, stored, or used.  To
503	   provide assurance about the requested key pair, the relevant claims
504	   must be bound to the CSR's public key in a cryptographically
505	   verifiable manner, and appraisal must establish that they support the
506	   claimed properties of that key pair.  Including a requester-supplied
507	   public key or its hash in a signed platform attestation does not, by
508	   itself, demonstrate that the corresponding private key was generated
509	   or is protected in the attested environment.

511	   Regardless of the topological model, when a CA/RA relies on
512	   attestation statements to establish a property of the CSR's public
513	   key or the corresponding private key, it MUST ensure that this
514	   binding has been validated.  The CA/RA may rely on a trusted Verifier
515	   to perform this validation but remains responsible for ensuring that
516	   it has been performed.  The binding may identify the public key
517	   directly or through a cryptographically verifiable reference, as
518	   defined by the applicable attestation format.

520	   When Evidence is used to establish such a key property, validation
521	   includes establishing that the claims refer to the requested key pair
522	   and support the properties on which the CA/RA relies.  This appraisal
523	   may be performed by a CA/RA acting as a Verifier or by an external
524	   trusted Verifier.  When Attestation Results are used for this
525	   purpose, the CA/RA MUST authenticate the results and establish that
526	   they apply to the CSR's public key and confirm the required
527	   properties based on a binding validated by the trusted Verifier.
528	   These requirements apply whether Evidence or Attestation Results are
529	   carried in the CSR or obtained during its processing.  For the code-
530	   signing use case described in Section 1, the CA/RA therefore needs
531	   assurance that the claims of HSM generation and protection apply to
532	   the key pair for which the certificate is requested.

534	   If no validated binding is available, the CA/RA MUST NOT treat
535	   attestation statements as establishing properties of the CSR's public
536	   key or the corresponding private key.  The CA/RA's issuance policy
537	   determines whether to reject the CSR, obtain additional evidence, or
538	   issue a certificate based on other acceptable information.  The
539	   absence of a validated binding does not, by itself, require rejection
540	   under this specification.  However, an issuance policy that requires
541	   attested properties of the requested key cannot be satisfied by
542	   attestation statements that lack such validated binding.

544	   Multiple attestation statements from multiple sources, as envisioned
545	   in [RFC9334], can introduce additional complications as shown in the
546	   following example.

548	   For example, a CA may have an issuance policy that requires key
549	   generation in an HSM on a company-owned platform in a known good
550	   state.  The CSR might contain three AttestationStatements originated
551	   by three different attesters:

553	   1.  that a key pair was generated in an HSM;
554	   2.  that a particular platform is company-owned; and

556	   3.  that a particular platform was in a known good state (e.g, up to
557	       date on patches, etc.).

559	   While each of these attestations may be independently correct, the
560	   CA/RA is responsible for confirming the attestations apply in concert
561	   to the public key in the CSR.  That is, the CA/RA must analyze the
562	   attestations to ensure that:

564	   1.  the attestation of HSM generation by AttestationStatement 1
565	       applies to the public key in the CSR;

567	   2.  the attestation of company ownership by AttestationStatement 2
568	       applies to the platform that contains the HSM; and

570	   3.  the attestation that a platform was in a known good state by
571	       AttestationStatement 3 applies to the platform that contains the
572	       HSM.

574	6.2.  Freshness

576	   To avoid replay attacks, the CA/RA may choose to ignore attestation
577	   statements that are stale, or whose freshness cannot be determined.
578	   Mechanisms to address freshness and their application to the RATS
579	   topological models are discussed in [RFC9334].  Other mechanisms for
580	   determining freshness may be used as the CA/RA deems appropriate.
581	   When CSRs are embedded within certificate management protocols such
582	   as Certificate Management Protocol (CMP) [RFC9810], Enrollment over
583	   Secure Transport (EST) [RFC7030], and Certificate Management over CMS
584	   (CMC) [RFC10002], these protocols can supply an Attester with a
585	   nonce, for example.  Further details are specified in
586	   [I-D.ietf-lamps-attestation-freshness].

588	6.3.  Resource Exhaustion

590	   CSRs are untrusted input to a CA/RA, and processing attestation
591	   bundles may consume substantial resources through parsing,
592	   cryptographic verification, certification path building and
593	   validation, and requests to external Verifiers or other services.  An
594	   attacker can exploit these costs to cause resource exhaustion, even
595	   with a syntactically valid bundle.

597	   Implementations SHOULD impose limits appropriate to their deployment
598	   on the total bundle size, the number, type and size of attestation
599	   statements and certificates, parsing depth, and the computational
600	   effort spent processing a request.  These limits SHOULD also bound
601	   certification path building, as well as the number, duration, and
602	   concurrency of external requests.  Implementations SHOULD enforce
603	   limits as early as practicable.  When a limit is exceeded,
604	   implementations SHOULD stop the affected processing and release
605	   resources allocated to it.  An attestation statement whose
606	   verification has not completed successfully MUST NOT be treated as
607	   successfully verified.

609	6.4.  Additional Security Considerations

611	   In addition to the security considerations listed here, implementers
612	   should be familiar with the security considerations of the
613	   specifications on which this specification depends: PKCS#10
614	   [RFC2986], CRMF [RFC4211], as well as general security concepts
615	   relating to remote attestation.  Many of these concepts are discussed
616	   in Section 6 of [RFC9334], Section 7 of [RFC9334], Section 9 of
617	   [RFC9334], Section 11 of [RFC9334], and Section 12 of [RFC9334].
618	   Implementers should also be aware of any security considerations
619	   relating to the specific attestation formats being carried within the
620	   CSR.

622	7.  Privacy Considerations

624	   Attestation statements and accompanying certificates can disclose
625	   information about a device, its software and hardware environment,
626	   and its owner or users.  These statements may contain Evidence,
627	   Endorsements, or Attestation Results.  Attesters SHOULD consider how
628	   much information is appropriate to disclose to a given CA.  Examples
629	   include hardware identifiers, software versions, patch levels, and
630	   device ownership.  These disclosures occur during enrollment, even if
631	   no attestation data is copied into the issued certificate.

633	   Attestation formats may use stable device-identifying public keys,
634	   certificates, device IDs, or other identifiers.  These values can
635	   enable a CA/RA or Verifier to link multiple CSRs to the same device
636	   even when the CSR public key, subject name, or enrollment account
637	   changes.  Such linkability can affect privacy even when the
638	   individual attested claims are not otherwise sensitive.

640	   A CA/RA that forwards attestation statements or accompanying
641	   certificates to an external Verifier discloses this information to
642	   another party.  That party may also be able to correlate requests
643	   across enrollments or across CAs.  The privacy assessment of a
644	   deployment therefore needs to cover the entire enrollment and
645	   attestation verification path, including third-party processing,
646	   retention, and logging.

648	   Certificate requesters, CAs/RAs, and Verifiers SHOULD minimize the
649	   claims contained in attestation statements and the accompanying
650	   certificates they disclose, process, and retain to those needed for
651	   the applicable issuance policy.  Where supported by the attestation
652	   technology and compatible with that policy, deployments SHOULD use
653	   mechanisms that reduce device linkability, such as privacy-preserving
654	   attestation procedures.  Such mechanisms do not eliminate correlation
655	   through other disclosed information.  Enrollment protocols and
656	   communication with external Verifiers SHOULD provide confidentiality
657	   protection and authenticate recipients.  Transport confidentiality
658	   protects against observers but does not prevent correlation by the
659	   recipients themselves.  Access to stored attestation statements and
660	   accompanying certificates, including copies in logs, SHOULD be
661	   restricted, and retention SHOULD be limited to the period needed for
662	   issuance and applicable audit requirements.

664	   Attestation statements are intended as additional information in the
665	   issuance process.  It is NOT RECOMMENDED for a CA to copy attestation
666	   statements into the published certificate.  CAs that choose to
667	   republish attestation statements or claims contained in them in
668	   certificates SHOULD review the contents and omit sensitive or
669	   identifying information.

671	   Further discussion of remote attestation privacy risks and
672	   mitigations is provided in Section 11 of [RFC9334].  Deployments
673	   should also consider the privacy considerations of the specific
674	   attestation formats they use.
